\begin{abstract}
Cross-silo federated graph learning for credit scoring, bail assessment and anti-money-laundering must satisfy three requirements simultaneously: demographic fairness, differential privacy, and resilience to malicious participants. Every deployed fairness-aware federated aggregator we are aware of steers on disparity statistics that clients report about themselves, which turns fairness steering into an attack surface: a participant that reports zero disparity while uploading a poisoned update can capture the aggregate. TrustFedGNN closes that surface. On the client, Fairness-Sensitive Edge Reweighting attenuates cross-group attention, and Fairness-Targeted Gradient Decomposition privatises only the two-dimensional group-mean statistic, obtaining $(\epsilon,\delta)$-differential privacy at $O(1)$ noise rather than the $O(\sqrt{P})$ of full-parameter DP-SGD. On the server, FU-Shapley scores each client by the alignment of its update with a target gradient the server computes on its own holdout split, then gates those scores onto the probability simplex; no self-reported field enters the weights. We prove metadata immunity and confirm it empirically: a falsifying client leaves the weight vector bit-identical ($\lVert\Delta \bm{w}\rVert_\infty = 0.0000$) while capturing $86.2\%$ of a Frank--Wolfe aggregate under the identical attack. Four algebraic properties are machine-checked in Lean 4. Across four demographic benchmarks and two large-scale graphs, TrustFedGNN attains the best AUC among seven external baselines on Pokec-z ($p = 0.0020$ after Holm--Bonferroni correction, winning on all ten seeds), and is statistically indistinguishable from all of them on a synthetic $k$-nearest-neighbour tabular graph. We report seven pre-registered hypotheses that our own measurements refuted.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Graph neural networks (GNNs) are now the default model class for fraud detection, anti-money-laundering (AML) and credit assessment \cite{kipf2016semi,velickovic2017graph,dou2020enhancing}, because the entities in those domains are defined as much by their relations---transactions, shared devices, social ties---as by their own attributes. In cross-institutional settings, however, data-protection regimes such as the EU General Data Protection Regulation forbid pooling those graphs into one repository. The resulting setting is \emph{cross-silo horizontal federated graph learning}: $K$ institutions each hold an induced subgraph, inter-silo edges are severed, and only model updates cross the boundary \cite{mcmahan2017communication,zhang2021federated}.

Deploying such a system in a high-stakes domain requires three properties at once, and each pair of them is in tension.

\paragraph{Structural bias against group fairness.} Nodes sharing a sensitive attribute are densely interconnected---demographic homophily---so recursive neighbourhood aggregation propagates and amplifies historical bias \cite{dai2021say,agarwal2021nifty}.

\paragraph{Group fairness against differential privacy.} Enforcing a fairness constraint requires the server to observe some disparity signal, and that signal leaks group membership. The textbook remedy, DP-SGD over the full parameter vector \cite{abadi2016deep}, injects noise scaling as $O(\sqrt{P})$ and destroys GNN utility: in our own ablation it costs $0.2016$ AUC (Section~\ref{sec:res_privacy}).

\paragraph{Fairness steering against Byzantine resilience.} This is the tension we consider unresolved, and it motivates the paper. Fairness-aware federated aggregators---FairFed \cite{ezzeldin2023fairfed}, PUFFLE \cite{corbucci2024puffle}, F$^2$GNN \cite{f2gnn2023}, FairGFL \cite{fairgfl2026}---compute aggregation weights from disparity statistics \emph{that each client reports about itself}. Nothing in those protocols verifies the report. An adversary may therefore transmit a poisoned update while declaring $\widehat{\dpd}_k = 0$, and the fairness mechanism will reward it precisely because it claims to be fair. Kasyap et al.\ \cite{kasyap2025attack} and EAB-FL \cite{eabfl2024} demonstrate this class of attack; classical Byzantine defences \cite{blanchard2017krum,yin2018byzantine,cao2021fltrust} do not address it, because they screen the parameter update and never examine the metadata channel at all.

\subsection{Contributions}

TrustFedGNN removes the metadata channel rather than trying to secure it. Its server computes the reference signal it needs \emph{itself}, on a holdout split no client can touch, and derives aggregation weights from how well each client's update aligns with that reference. A client's report about its own fairness is never read, so falsifying it is mathematically inert.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Pillar C1 --- privacy-aware fairness feedback at the client (Section~\ref{sec:client}).} Fairness-Sensitive Edge Reweighting (FSER) attenuates attention on cross-group edges before the softmax. Fairness-Targeted Gradient Decomposition (FTGD) projects the update orthogonally to the fairness-ascent direction and injects Gaussian noise into the released two-dimensional statistic $(\mu_0,\mu_1)$ only, computed on a sensitive-blind forward pass. This yields a certified $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP release at $O(1)$ noise, at a measured utility cost of $0.0017$ AUC against $0.2016$ for full-parameter DP-SGD---a factor of $115$.

\item \textbf{Pillar C2 --- metadata-independent contribution aggregation at the server (Section~\ref{sec:server}).} FU-Shapley scores client $k$ by $\varphi_k = \langle g_k, g_{\mathrm{target}}\rangle / \lVert g_{\mathrm{target}}\rVert$, where $g_{\mathrm{target}} = g_{\mathrm{task}}^{\mathrm{srv}} + \alpha\, g_{\mathrm{fair}}^{\mathrm{srv}}$ is computed on the server holdout; scores are smoothed by an exponential moving average and gated onto the simplex by a rectifier. Theorem~\ref{thm:metadata_immunity} establishes that the weights are a function of the transmitted updates alone. Empirically, a client that falsifies its report leaves the weight vector bit-identical, while the same falsification hands it $86.2\%$ of a Frank--Wolfe aggregate (Table~\ref{tab:metadata_immunity}).

\item \textbf{Pillar C3 --- evidence, with its boundaries stated (Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:limitations}).} Four demographic-fairness benchmarks and two large-scale graphs using proxy groups; seventeen implemented baselines; ten seeds with paired Wilcoxon tests under Holm--Bonferroni correction; four algebraic properties machine-checked in Lean 4. We also report seven pre-registered hypotheses that our measurements refuted, including the utility attribution of FSER itself.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Our central claim is deliberately bounded, and we state it once, in the form we can defend against every table in this paper:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Among the methods surveyed here, TrustFedGNN is the only one that holds all four trust properties simultaneously at an acceptable level, and the only one whose fairness-aware aggregation does not depend on client-reported metadata. On individual axes, specialised methods still score better, and we report every such case.}
\end{quote}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

We organise the literature along the five axes that the problem forces together.

\subsection{Fair graph neural networks}
Centralised fair-GNN methods intervene at the representation, the input, or the neighbourhood. FairGNN \cite{dai2021say} trains an adversarial discriminator to purge sensitive information from embeddings under partial attribute observation; NIFTY \cite{agarwal2021nifty} adds counterfactual and Lipschitz-stability regularisation; EDITS \cite{dong2022edits} learns a debiasing transformation of features and adjacency before message passing; FairSIN \cite{yang2024fairsin} neutralises sensitive information by injecting fairness-aware auxiliary features. More recent work recasts fairness as class-and-group rebalancing \cite{li2024fairgb}, seeks representations invariant across unseen sensitive attributes \cite{zhu2024fairinv}, disentangles then selectively debiases latent factors \cite{lee2025dabgnn}, or regularises the divergence between group score distributions rather than their means \cite{fnrgnn2025}. All assume centralised access to the full graph.

\subsection{Fairness in federated learning}
Two distinct notions travel under the name. \emph{Performance uniformity} equalises accuracy across clients: $q$-FedAvg \cite{li2020fair} reweights toward high-loss participants. \emph{Group fairness} equalises outcomes across sensitive-attribute groups irrespective of client boundaries; this is the notion we adopt. In that second family, FairFed \cite{ezzeldin2023fairfed} adjusts aggregation weights from client-reported disparity gaps, PUFFLE \cite{corbucci2024puffle} co-tunes a privacy budget and a fairness coefficient, FedFACT \cite{fedfact2025} and FDP-Fair \cite{xue2026fdpfair,ling2024feddp} post-process or dualise the constraint, and the PoPETs-track framework of Bendoukha et al.\ \cite{bendoukha2025popets} combines secure aggregation with fairness-aware selection. F$^2$GNN \cite{f2gnn2023} is the closest structure-aware federated attempt, and FairGFL \cite{fairgfl2026} and FedGraph-Fair \cite{fedgraphfair2026} are recent federated graph systems evaluated on financial data; both appear as baselines in Section~\ref{sec:results}. The common structural weakness is stated above: the fairness signal is self-reported and unverified.

\subsection{Differential privacy and Byzantine robustness}
DP-SGD \cite{abadi2016deep,geyer2017differentially,mcmahan2018learning} clips and perturbs the full update, accounted through Rényi DP \cite{mironov2017renyi} and converted to $(\epsilon,\delta)$ by the analytic formula of Canonne, Kamath and Steinke \cite{canonne2020discrete}. Because $P \ge 10^5$ for even compact GNNs, isotropic noise is ruinous---quantified as arm M4 in Section~\ref{sec:res_attribution}. Byzantine-robust rules screen updates geometrically: Krum \cite{blanchard2017krum}, coordinate-wise median and trimmed mean \cite{yin2018byzantine}, FLTrust \cite{cao2021fltrust}. A substantial literature shows these can be evaded by adversaries that stay inside the benign geometric envelope \cite{baruch2019little,xie2019fall,mhamdi2018hidden,bagdasaryan2020backdoor}. None of it considers an adversary whose parameter update is unremarkable but whose \emph{auxiliary fairness metadata} is fabricated---the threat we formalise in Section~\ref{sec:threat}.

\subsection{Contribution valuation}
Our aggregator descends from the contribution-valuation literature rather than from constrained optimisation. GTG-Shapley \cite{liu2022gtg} estimates a client's marginal contribution as a cooperative-game value; CGSV \cite{xu2021gradient} scores by cosine alignment between client and aggregate gradients; further schemes translate contribution into differentiated rewards \cite{shi2024fedfaim,tastan2024redefining,lo2025fairwag,yu2026equfl}. TrustFedGNN's departure is the reference against which alignment is measured: CGSV aligns against the aggregate of the very updates being scored, which an adversarial majority can shift, whereas we align against a target gradient computed on data the server holds privately, and we make that target explicitly bi-objective so that fairness contribution is scored, not merely utility. Exact Shapley computation is $O(2^K)$ and infeasible here; we return to what our $O(KP)$ surrogate does and does not inherit from it in Section~\ref{sec:res_shapley}.

\section{Problem Formulation and Threat Model}
\label{sec:formulation}

\subsection{Cross-silo federated graph learning}
Let $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E},\bm{X})$ be partitioned across $K$ institutions. Client $k$ holds $\mathcal{G}_k = (\mathcal{V}_k,\mathcal{E}_k,\bm{X}_k)$ with features $\bm{X}_k \in \R^{n_k \times d}$, binary labels $\bm{y}_k$, and binary sensitive attributes $\bm{s}_k$; inter-silo edges are severed and cannot be exchanged. The federation minimises
\begin{equation}
\min_{\theta \in \R^P} \; F(\theta) = \sum_{k=1}^K \frac{n_k}{N}\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}^{(k)}(\theta)
\quad \text{s.t.} \quad \dpd(\theta) \le \tau ,
\label{eq:global_opt}
\end{equation}
with $\dpd(\theta) = \lvert \E[\hat{y}_v \mid s_v = 1] - \E[\hat{y}_v \mid s_v = 0]\rvert$ and $\tau$ an operator-set tolerance. We report the \emph{hard} disparity $\dpd_{\mathrm{hard}}$, computed on thresholded labels, in all tables; the differentiable surrogate $\dpd_{\mathrm{soft}}$ on probability scores is used only inside the loss. The two are never mixed.

\subsection{Threat model}
\label{sec:threat}
Two adversaries operate simultaneously.

\paragraph{Honest-but-curious server.} It follows the protocol but attempts to infer $\bm{s}_k$ from everything it receives: the released fairness statistic and the raw parameter update $g_k = \theta_t - \theta_k$. We treat these as two distinct channels throughout, because our guarantee covers one and not the other.

\paragraph{Adaptive stealth fairness poisoner.} Up to $f < K/2$ clients are malicious and omniscient. Such a client constructs a poisoning update $g_{\mathrm{poison}}$ that maximises disparity, then projects it into a safe ball around the benign median so that geometric screens do not fire,
\begin{equation}
g_{\mathrm{stealth}} = \mathrm{med}(G) + \min\!\left(1, \frac{r_{\mathrm{safe}}}{\lVert g_{\mathrm{poison}} - \mathrm{med}(G)\rVert_2}\right)\!\left(g_{\mathrm{poison}} - \mathrm{med}(G)\right),
\label{eq:stealth}
\end{equation}
with $r_{\mathrm{safe}} = 0.85 \cdot R_{\mathrm{med}}$, and \emph{simultaneously falsifies its metadata}, declaring $\widehat{\dpd}_k = 0.0$ and $\mathrm{Perf}_k = 0.99$. The falsification is the operative part: it is what turns a fairness-steering aggregator from a defence into a lever.

One communication round proceeds through six stages, illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} and consolidated in Algorithm~\ref{alg:trustfedgnn}.

\subsection{Client-side mechanisms (Pillar C1)}
\label{sec:client}

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{$S_1$:}{S1:} Fairness-Sensitive Edge Reweighting}
At GAT layer $l$, attention logits are attenuated on edges that cross the sensitive boundary:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{e}^{(l)}_{vu} = e^{(l)}_{vu} - \beta^{(l)} \cdot \mathbb{I}(s_v \neq s_u) \cdot \max\!\big(0, \cos(\bm{h}^{(l-1)}_v, \bm{h}^{(l-1)}_u)\big),
\label{eq:fser}
\end{equation}
with $\beta^{(l)} \ge 0$ learned and clamped to $[0,5]$ to keep the softmax numerically well-posed. The penalty fires only where two conditions hold at once: the edge crosses groups, and the endpoint representations are already similar. Its intended role is to damp the specific pathway by which homophily converts into predictive disparity, while leaving task-relevant homophilic structure untouched.

Equation~\eqref{eq:fser} has a property worth stating because it determines where the mechanism can possibly act. If a graph contains no cross-group edges, the indicator is identically zero and FSER reduces exactly to plain GAT attention---a mathematical no-op, not a weak effect. This is why the Elliptic Bitcoin graph, whose temporal proxy groups yield $h_s = 1.0000$ by construction, cannot serve as a measurement point for FSER at all (Section~\ref{sec:datasets}).

\begin{quote}
We do not claim a causal separation between demographic similarity and behavioural similarity; FSER is a learned heuristic, and Section~\ref{sec:res_attribution} reports that its measured contribution to utility on our benchmarks is null.
\end{quote}

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{$S_2$:}{S2:} Fairness-Targeted Gradient Decomposition}
Let $g_{\mathrm{total}} = \nabla_\theta(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fair}})$ and $g_{\mathrm{fair}} = \nabla_\theta(\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fair}})$. FTGD removes from the step any component along the fairness-ascent direction:
\begin{equation}
g^{\perp}_{\mathrm{task}} = g_{\mathrm{total}} - \frac{\langle g_{\mathrm{total}}, g_{\mathrm{fair}}\rangle}{\lVert g_{\mathrm{fair}}\rVert^2 + \varepsilon}\, g_{\mathrm{fair}} .
\label{eq:ftgd}
\end{equation}
The projection is guarded by a minimum-norm threshold on $\lVert g_{\mathrm{fair}}\rVert$ rather than an arbitrary $\varepsilon$, so a vanishing fairness gradient near convergence does not amplify numerical noise.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{$S_3$:}{S3:} Statistic-level differential privacy}
The sensitive attribute enters the objective through exactly two scalars, the group prediction means $\mu_g = \lvert\mathcal{V}_{k,g}\rvert^{-1}\sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{k,g}} \hat{y}_v$. The task loss does not depend on $\bm{s}_k$ at all. This is the observation the mechanism rests on: privatise the two scalars, not the $P$-dimensional parameter vector,
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mu}_g = \mu_g + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\mathrm{DP}}^2), \qquad
\widetilde{\dpd}_k = \lvert \tilde{\mu}_1 - \tilde{\mu}_0 \rvert .
\label{eq:dp}
\end{equation}

One subtlety is load-bearing. Because FSER consumes $\bm{s}$ inside the attention mechanism, the network's prediction for node $v$ depends on the sensitive attributes of $v$'s whole $L$-hop neighbourhood, not on $s_v$ alone---which would invalidate the per-node sensitivity bound of Assumption~\ref{ass:sensitivity}. TrustFedGNN therefore computes the released statistic on a second, \emph{sensitive-blind} forward pass, in which the FSER penalty is disabled, while training continues to use the real $\bm{s}$. The cost is one extra forward pass per round; the benefit is that Proposition~\ref{prop:dp} applies to the quantity actually released.

\subsection{Server-side aggregation (Pillar C2)}
\label{sec:server}

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{$S_4$:}{S4:} FU-Shapley}
The server maintains a holdout split $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{val}}$ that no client contributes to or observes. On it, it computes its own bi-objective reference direction
\begin{equation}
g_{\mathrm{target}} = g_{\mathrm{task}}^{\mathrm{srv}} + \alpha\, g_{\mathrm{fair}}^{\mathrm{srv}},
\qquad
g_{\mathrm{fair}}^{\mathrm{srv}} = \nabla_\theta \big(\mu_0^{\mathrm{val}} - \mu_1^{\mathrm{val}}\big)^2 ,
\label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 0.1$. Three details of Eq.~\eqref{eq:target} are easy to get wrong and change the mechanism's meaning. The sign is \emph{positive}: $g_{\mathrm{fair}}^{\mathrm{srv}}$ ascends the disparity penalty, so a client that reduces disparity aligns positively with it and earns positive fairness credit; a minus sign would penalise exactly the clients one wants to reward. The server-side surrogate is the \emph{squared} gap, which is smooth and whose gradient attenuates as fairness is achieved, whereas clients retain the non-smooth $\lvert\mu_0 - \mu_1\rvert$; the asymmetry is deliberate. Finally, coordinates are laid out following \texttt{state\_dict()} rather than \texttt{parameters()}, so that the scoring inner product is aligned coordinate-for-coordinate with the flattened pseudo-gradients, buffers without gradients being zero-filled.

Client $k$'s instantaneous contribution and its round-normalised form are
\begin{equation}
\varphi_k^{\mathrm{raw}} = \langle g_k, g_{\mathrm{target}}\rangle,
\qquad
\varphi_k = \frac{\varphi_k^{\mathrm{raw}}}{\lVert g_{\mathrm{target}}\rVert + 10^{-8}} .
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
Normalising by $\lVert g_{\mathrm{target}}\rVert$ removes the scale drift that would otherwise occur as $g_{\mathrm{task}}^{\mathrm{srv}} \to 0$ near convergence, and---unlike a $z$-score, which re-centres and therefore gates roughly half the clients at every round regardless of behaviour---it preserves the sign, so that the rectifier below retains its intended meaning of ``actively harmful'' rather than ``below average''. Scores are smoothed across rounds,
\begin{equation}
\bar{\varphi}_k^{(t)} = \beta_{\mathrm{ema}}\, \bar{\varphi}_k^{(t-1)} + (1 - \beta_{\mathrm{ema}})\, \varphi_k^{(t)},
\qquad \beta_{\mathrm{ema}} = 0.9 ,
\label{eq:ema}
\end{equation}
and gated onto the simplex with an explicit null-player mask,
\begin{equation}
w_k = \frac{\max(0,\bar{\varphi}_k)\cdot\mathbb{I}(g_k \neq \bm{0})}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}\max(0,\bar{\varphi}_j)\cdot\mathbb{I}(g_j \neq \bm{0})} .
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Finiteness contract.} A non-finite score is an absorbing state of the recursion in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ema}, and it has a security consequence that is not obvious. Under a sign-flip attack the adversary's gradient can explode, making $\varphi$ non-finite; since \texttt{nan > 0} evaluates to false, a naive implementation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate} would silently degenerate to uniform averaging and hand the attacker its full $1/K$ share. TrustFedGNN therefore retains the previous EMA value for any coordinate whose score is non-finite, and zeroes it at the first round---an undefined contribution earns no credit. When \emph{every} score is non-positive the rule falls back to uniform weighting over active clients, and the two causes are logged distinctly: \texttt{degenerate\_nan} (the measurement failed) versus \texttt{degenerate\_nonpos} (all clients genuinely harmed the objective).

\paragraph{Robust variant.} \texttt{robust\_fu\_shapley} prepends a screening step, discarding the $f$ updates furthest from the coordinate-wise median before scoring the survivors. Section~\ref{sec:res_byzantine} shows this is not redundant: the rectifier alone suffices against sign-flip and fairness poisoning but is defeated by gradient scaling, which the screen closes.

\paragraph{Explainability by construction.} Bilinearity of the inner product splits each score into an audit trail without any additional computation, $\varphi_k = \varphi_k^{\mathrm{util}} + \alpha\,\varphi_k^{\mathrm{fair}}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:decomposition}). A positive $\varphi_k^{\mathrm{fair}}$ means client $k$ moved the global model toward parity. The federation operator therefore obtains a per-client, per-round statement of \emph{why} a participant was weighted as it was---a governance artifact rather than a post-hoc rationalisation.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{$S_5$:}{S5:} Global update and trust layer}
The server applies $\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta\sum_k w_k g_k$ and measures MC-dropout predictive variance \cite{gal2016dropout}, expected calibration error, a composite trust score, and the aggregation-weight total variation $\Omega_w = \sum_t \lVert \bm{w}^{(t)} - \bm{w}^{(t-1)}\rVert_1$.

\begin{remark}[Reading $\Omega_w$]
$\Omega_w > 0$ is a design consequence, not instability. FedAvg attains $\Omega_w = 0$ because its weights are fixed sample proportions and carry no information about behaviour; any rule that re-scores participants each round must move. The meaningful comparison is against another adaptive rule, and there FU-Shapley is $27\times$ steadier than the Frank--Wolfe baseline (Table~\ref{tab:weight_stability}).
\end{remark}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{TrustFedGNN: one communication round}
\label{alg:trustfedgnn}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Global parameters $\theta_t$; server holdout $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{val}}$; fairness weight $\alpha$; EMA factor $\beta_{\mathrm{ema}}$; DP scale $\sigma_{\mathrm{DP}}$; EMA state $\bar{\bm{\varphi}}^{(t-1)}$
\ENSURE Updated parameters $\theta_{t+1}$, updated EMA state
\STATE Server broadcasts $\theta_t$ to all clients \COMMENT{$S_0$}
\FOR{each client $k$ \textbf{in parallel}}
    \STATE Train $E$ local epochs with FSER attention, Eq.~\eqref{eq:fser} \COMMENT{$S_1$}
    \STATE At each step, take the FTGD direction $g^{\perp}_{\mathrm{task}}$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ftgd} \COMMENT{$S_2$}
    \STATE Recompute $(\mu_0,\mu_1)$ on an $s$-blind pass; release $\tilde{\mu}_g$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:dp} \COMMENT{$S_3$}
    \STATE Upload $g_k = \theta_t - \theta_k$, and $\widetilde{\dpd}_k$ \emph{for logging only}
\ENDFOR
\STATE Server computes $g_{\mathrm{target}}$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{val}}$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:target} \COMMENT{$S_4$}
\STATE \textit{(robust variant)} discard the $f$ updates furthest from $\mathrm{med}(\{g_k\})$
\STATE Score $\varphi_k$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:score}; smooth by Eq.~\eqref{eq:ema}; gate by Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate}
\STATE $\theta_{t+1} \leftarrow \theta_t - \eta \sum_k w_k g_k$ \COMMENT{$S_5$}
\RETURN $\theta_{t+1}, \bar{\bm{\varphi}}^{(t)}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{The BFWA baseline}
\label{sec:bfwa_baseline}
For comparison we also implement Bi-objective Frank--Wolfe Aggregation (BFWA), which treats Eq.~\eqref{eq:global_opt} as a constrained programme over the simplex, $\max_{\bm{w}\in\Delta_K}\sum_k w_k\mathrm{Perf}_k$ subject to $\sum_k w_k \widetilde{\dpd}_k \le \tau$, solved by Frank--Wolfe steps with a persistent dual multiplier ascending on the constraint residual. BFWA is the natural formalisation of ``steer the aggregate toward a fairness budget'', and it is exactly the design whose reliance on $\widetilde{\dpd}_k$ this paper argues against. We report it as a baseline throughout, never as a component of TrustFedGNN, and Theorem~\ref{thm:bfwa_convergence} characterises its dual iterates for completeness.

\subsection{Complexity}
\label{sec:complexity}
Uplink per client per round is one parameter vector plus two scalars, identical to FedAvg up to $O(1)$, and independent of $K$---in contrast to secure-aggregation protocols whose pairwise masking costs $O(K^2)$. Client-side overhead is $O(\lvert\mathcal{E}_k\rvert d)$ for the FSER penalty, the same order as the attention it modifies, plus $O(P)$ for the FTGD projection and one additional forward pass for the $s$-blind statistic. Server-side scoring costs one backward pass on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{val}}$ plus $K$ inner products of length $P$, i.e.\ $O(KP)$, against the $O(2^K P)$ of exact Shapley evaluation. Measured end-to-end this is $2.06\times$ FedAvg-GCN on Pokec-z (Table~\ref{tab:cost}).

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

Four of the results below are machine-checked. We are precise about what that means: the Lean 4 files formalise the \emph{algebraic identities} over an abstract inner-product structure whose axioms are supplied explicitly, and each compiles with exit code $0$ and no \texttt{sorry}. They certify the algebra, not the statistical or learning-theoretic content of the surrounding claims.

\subsection{Privacy of the released statistic}

\begin{assumption}[Bounded predictions]\label{ass:bounded}
The output layer is a sigmoid, so $\hat{y}_v \in (0,1)$ for every node.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Public group sizes]\label{ass:counts}
The counts $n_{k,0},n_{k,1}$ are public, with $n_{\min} = \min(n_{k,0},n_{k,1}) \ge 1$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Swap adjacency]\label{ass:adjacency}
Two local datasets are adjacent if they share node set and features and differ by \emph{exchanging} the sensitive attribute between exactly two nodes of opposite groups. Group sizes are thereby held fixed, consistently with Assumption~\ref{ass:counts}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Sensitivity]\label{ass:sensitivity}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:adjacency}, $\lVert\bm{\mu}(\mathcal{D}) - \bm{\mu}(\mathcal{D}')\rVert_2 \le \Delta_{\bm{\mu}} = \sqrt{n_{k,0}^{-2} + n_{k,1}^{-2}} \le \sqrt{2}/n_{\min}$, and $\bm{\mu}$ is computed on an $s$-blind forward pass.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}
Assumption~\ref{ass:adjacency} is a swap rather than a toggle for a reason. Toggling $s_v$ would change the denominators $n_{k,g}$ as well as the numerators, so the bound $1/n_{k,g}$ would not follow, and it would contradict Assumption~\ref{ass:counts}. The swap formulation keeps the group sizes fixed and makes the sensitivity derivable rather than assumed.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Statistic-level DP]\label{prop:dp}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:bounded}--\ref{ass:sensitivity}, releasing $\tilde{\bm{\mu}}_k = \bm{\mu}_k + \bm{\xi}_k$ with $\bm{\xi}_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\mathrm{DP}}^2 \bm{I}_2)$ satisfies $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP with respect to $\bm{s}_k$ whenever $\sigma_{\mathrm{DP}} \ge \Delta_{\bm{\mu}}\sqrt{2\ln(1.25/\delta)}/\epsilon$. The scalar $\widetilde{\dpd}_k$ inherits the same guarantee at no additional budget.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exchanging the attribute between two nodes of opposite groups moves one bounded prediction out of each group mean and one in, so by Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded} the change in $\mu_g$ is at most $1/n_{k,g}$, giving the $\ell_2$ bound of Assumption~\ref{ass:sensitivity} with denominators fixed by Assumption~\ref{ass:adjacency}. The Gaussian mechanism \cite[Thm.~A.1]{dwork2014algorithmic} applied to a query of that sensitivity yields the stated $\sigma_{\mathrm{DP}}$. Since $\widetilde{\dpd}_k = \lvert\tilde{\mu}_1 - \tilde{\mu}_0\rvert$ is a data-independent function of an already-privatised release, post-processing \cite[Prop.~2.1]{dwork2014algorithmic} transfers the guarantee unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Multi-round composition]\label{cor:rdp}
Over $T = R \times E$ releases, the protocol satisfies $(\alpha, T\alpha\Delta_{\bm{\mu}}^2/2\sigma_{\mathrm{DP}}^2)$-RDP for every $\alpha > 1$ \cite{mironov2017renyi}, converting to $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP through the analytic bound of Canonne, Kamath and Steinke \cite{canonne2020discrete},
\begin{equation}
\epsilon = \min_{\alpha>1}\left[\frac{T\alpha\Delta_{\bm{\mu}}^2}{2\sigma_{\mathrm{DP}}^2} + \log\!\Big(1-\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\Big) + \frac{\log(1/\delta) - \log\alpha}{\alpha - 1}\right].
\label{eq:rdp}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\noindent The sign of the $\log\alpha$ term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rdp} is negative. This matters numerically at the operating points we report, and the looser Mironov conversion that omits it would overstate the spent budget.

\begin{quote}
The certificate covers the released statistic sequence. It does not cover the model update, and Section~\ref{sec:res_privacy} measures what remains exposed there.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Orthogonality of the transmitted update}

\begin{theorem}[Fairness-subspace non-leakage]\label{thm:non_leakage}
For $\lVert g_{\mathrm{fair}}\rVert \neq 0$, the FTGD output of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ftgd} satisfies $\langle g^{\perp}_{\mathrm{task}}, g_{\mathrm{fair}}\rangle \to 0$ as $\varepsilon \to 0$. Consequently no linear functional of $g^{\perp}_{\mathrm{task}}$ recovers a component along $g_{\mathrm{fair}}$, the only direction along which $\bm{s}_k$ enters the local objective by construction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Bilinearity gives $\langle g^{\perp}_{\mathrm{task}}, g_{\mathrm{fair}}\rangle = \langle g_{\mathrm{total}},g_{\mathrm{fair}}\rangle\,\varepsilon/(\lVert g_{\mathrm{fair}}\rVert^2+\varepsilon)$, which vanishes with $\varepsilon$. The exact identity ($\varepsilon = 0$) is machine-checked in \texttt{Orthogonal\allowbreak Projection.lean}. Since $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}$ is a function of features and task labels alone, $g_{\mathrm{task}}$ carries no direct dependence on $\bm{s}_k$, so every path from $\bm{s}_k$ into the transmitted vector factors through $\mathrm{span}(g_{\mathrm{fair}})$, which the projection removes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What Theorem~\ref{thm:non_leakage} does not say]
\label{rem:non_leakage_scope}
Orthogonality is a second-order geometric property and does not imply statistical independence. We therefore state the conclusion as the removal of a \emph{linear} channel, and make no mutual-information claim: a nonlinear probe may still exploit residual correlation between $g^{\perp}_{\mathrm{task}}$ and $\bm{s}_k$ that does not factor through $g_{\mathrm{fair}}$. Table~\ref{tab:update_attack} measures that residual channel directly, and finds it non-zero.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Metadata immunity}

\begin{theorem}[Metadata immunity of FU-Shapley]\label{thm:metadata_immunity}
Let a client report arbitrary values $\widehat{\dpd}_k$ and $\mathrm{Perf}_k$ while transmitting update $g_k$. The weight vector produced by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:target}--\eqref{eq:gate} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\bm{w} = \mathcal{F}\big(\{g_1,\dots,g_K\},\, g_{\mathrm{target}}\big),
\end{equation}
a function of the transmitted updates and the server-computed reference alone. Hence for any two reporting strategies that differ only in the declared values but induce the same $g_k$, the resulting weight vectors are identical. Moreover, if a malicious update satisfies $\langle g_{\mathrm{adv}}, g_{\mathrm{target}}\rangle \le 0$ persistently, then $\bar{\varphi}_{\mathrm{adv}} \le 0$ and the rectifier of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate} assigns $w_{\mathrm{adv}} = 0$ exactly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The declared fields appear in no term of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:target}--\eqref{eq:gate}: $g_{\mathrm{target}}$ is a function of $\theta_t$ and $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{val}}$, both server-side; $\varphi_k$ is an inner product of $g_k$ with $g_{\mathrm{target}}$; the EMA and the gate are functions of $\{\varphi_j\}$. Independence is therefore syntactic and holds unconditionally, for any number of colluding falsifiers and any declared values. For the second part, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ema} is a convex combination of non-positive terms and stays non-positive, and $\max(0,\cdot)$ maps it to zero; the null-player mask keeps the numerator at zero in the degenerate branch as well (Proposition~\ref{prop:null_player}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope]
The first part of Theorem~\ref{thm:metadata_immunity} is unconditional and closes the metadata channel completely. The second part is conditional on the adversary's update anti-aligning with the reference, which need not hold: a gradient-scaling adversary aligns \emph{positively} while inflating magnitude, and Section~\ref{sec:res_byzantine} shows the rectifier alone then admits $w_{\mathrm{adv}} = 0.2008$. This is why the robust variant exists, and why we do not claim invulnerability.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Well-posedness of the weight rule}

\begin{proposition}[Simplex validity]\label{prop:simplex}
Whenever at least one active client has $\bar{\varphi}_k > 0$, the weights of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate} satisfy $w_k \ge 0$ and $\sum_k w_k = 1$. Machine-checked in \texttt{Simplex\allowbreak Properties.lean}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Null player]\label{prop:null_player}
If $g_k = \bm{0}$ then $w_k = 0$ in every execution branch, including the degenerate fallback. Machine-checked in \texttt{Null\allowbreak Player.lean}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\varphi_k = \langle\bm{0},g_{\mathrm{target}}\rangle = 0$ propagates through Eq.~\eqref{eq:ema} to $\bar{\varphi}_k = 0$, and $\max(0,0) = 0$; the indicator $\mathbb{I}(g_k \neq \bm{0})$ additionally pins the numerator to zero in the uniform fallback, where a bare rectifier would otherwise pay a non-contributor an equal share.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Additive decomposition]\label{prop:decomposition}
$\varphi_k = \varphi_k^{\mathrm{util}} + \alpha\,\varphi_k^{\mathrm{fair}}$ with $\varphi_k^{\mathrm{util}} = \langle g_k, g_{\mathrm{task}}^{\mathrm{srv}}\rangle$ and $\varphi_k^{\mathrm{fair}} = \langle g_k, g_{\mathrm{fair}}^{\mathrm{srv}}\rangle$, by bilinearity. Machine-checked in \texttt{Linear\allowbreak Decomposition.lean}. This identity is the formal basis of the contribution audit of Section~\ref{sec:server} and of ablation arm M5 ($\alpha = 0$).
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Baseline: convergence of BFWA dual ascent}

\begin{theorem}[BFWA dual convergence]\label{thm:bfwa_convergence}
For the baseline programme of Section~\ref{sec:bfwa_baseline} with bounded $\mathrm{Perf}_k$ and $\widetilde{\dpd}_k \in [0,\dpd_{\max}]$, the dual function $D(\mu)$ is concave as a pointwise supremum of affine functions, its subgradient at $\mu^{(t)}$ is the constraint residual, and projected subgradient ascent with $\eta_\mu = O(1/\sqrt{t})$ gives $D(\bar{\mu}^{(T)}) - D(\mu^\star) = O(1/\sqrt{T})$, with average constraint violation vanishing at the same rate \cite{jaggi2013revisiting}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Two honest caveats]
Theorem~\ref{thm:bfwa_convergence} is a property of the \emph{baseline}, stated for completeness; it is not a guarantee about TrustFedGNN. And it does not transfer verbatim to our implementation of that baseline, which uses a constant $\eta_\mu = 0.1$ on a range-normalised residual rather than a decaying step. FU-Shapley itself has no analogous rate: its stability is an empirical property (Table~\ref{tab:weight_stability}), and we identify a convergence analysis for it as open work.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Scope of the theory}
Collecting the boundaries: the DP certificate covers the released statistic, not the update (Remark after Corollary~\ref{cor:rdp}); Theorem~\ref{thm:non_leakage} removes a linear channel, not all dependence (Remark~\ref{rem:non_leakage_scope}); Theorem~\ref{thm:metadata_immunity} closes the metadata channel unconditionally but bounds the parameter channel only under an anti-alignment condition; and no convergence rate is claimed for the proposed aggregator.

The accounting is exact and cheap: on German at $T = R\times E = 60$ releases the composed budget is $\epsilon = 7.9985 \le 8.0$ at $\delta = 10^{-4}$. Note that $\delta$ differs between the accounting campaign ($10^{-4}$) and the canonical training configuration ($10^{-5}$); we state which applies to which number rather than quoting a single $\delta$ for the paper.

Table~\ref{tab:update_attack} separates the two channels of Section~\ref{sec:threat}. On the statistic channel, an unnoised release is a perfect oracle for the sensitive attribute ($1.000$ attack AUC); FTGD reduces this to $0.498$ against a $0.500$ chance level, at a measured utility cost of $0.0017$ AUC. Full-parameter DP-SGD achieves a comparable reduction on the update channel but costs $0.2016$ AUC---a $115\times$ difference in the price of the same protection.

On the update channel, our guarantee does not reach, and the measurement says so: a linear probe on raw updates attains $0.645$ AUC when folds are grouped by client. The grouping matters and is easy to get wrong. An ungrouped probe reports $0.954$, but a client's majority attribute is constant across rounds, so ungrouped folds largely measure client re-identification rather than attribute inference; we report the grouped figure as the honest one and the ungrouped figure so the discrepancy is visible.

We do not claim end-to-end privacy. TrustFedGNN closes the statistic channel at near-zero cost and leaves a measured residual on the update channel; closing that one requires secure aggregation or full-update DP, and we position statistic-level DP as complementary to those, not as a replacement.

\subsection{Can a client capture the aggregate by lying about itself?}
\label{sec:res_metadata}

\input{tables/metadata_immunity.tex}

This is the paper's central claim and it admits an unusually clean test. We run the identical falsification---one client declares $\widehat{\dpd}_k = 0.0$ and $\mathrm{Perf}_k = 0.99$ while transmitting an unchanged update---against both aggregators and compare the weight vectors.

Under FU-Shapley the two weight vectors are identical to the last bit: $\lVert\Delta\bm{w}\rVert_\infty = 0.0000$, the falsifying client holding $0.2339$ whether it lies or not. Under BFWA the same client's weight moves from $0.0371$ to $0.8617$: by declaring itself perfectly fair and highly performant it captures $86.2\%$ of the aggregate. The robust variant is likewise bit-exact. This is Theorem~\ref{thm:metadata_immunity} made visible, and it is a difference in kind rather than degree---not a better-tuned defence, but the removal of the channel the attack needs.

\subsection{Would trusting the reported disparity even work, absent an adversary?}
\label{sec:res_slack}

\input{tables/revision/bfwa_slack.tex}

The previous subsection shows that a self-reported disparity can be falsified. Table~\ref{tab:bfwa_slack} makes a second, independent point: even with every client perfectly honest, the reported disparity is not a usable constraint signal once it has been privatised, because the noise required for the privacy guarantee swamps the quantity being constrained.

Measuring the slack $\Delta_\tau = \lvert \sum_k w_k \widehat{\dpd}_k - \sum_k w_k \dpd_k \rvert$ between reported and true aggregate disparity on German, we find $0.3526 \pm 0.4279$ at the weakest privacy setting tested ($\epsilon = 8.0$)---$705\%$ of the budget $\tau = 0.05$---rising to $2212\%$ at $\epsilon = 2.0$. Two consequences follow. An operator cannot read feasibility off the quantity a metadata-driven aggregator actually observes; and at this operating point the true aggregate disparity is itself $0.1350$, some $2.7\times$ the budget, so the constraint is violated in substance and not merely in measurement. Theorem~\ref{thm:bfwa_convergence} drives the \emph{average} violation to zero at $O(1/\sqrt{T})$, which is not a feasibility certificate at any finite round budget.

This is the deeper reason we route the fairness objective through the server's own holdout gradient rather than through a reported constraint: privacy and metadata-driven steering are not merely separately awkward, they are mutually destructive. Noising the statistic enough to protect it destroys its usefulness as a constraint; leaving it un-noised to preserve the constraint exposes the attribute (Table~\ref{tab:update_attack}, first row: $1.000$ attack AUC).

\subsection{What attacks does the defence withstand, and where does it break?}
\label{sec:res_byzantine}

\input{tables/two_tier_defense.tex}
\input{tables/revision/adaptive_poisoner.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:two_tier} reports adversary weight and utility under a $20\%$ Byzantine minority. Three things are visible, and the third is a failure.

First, the rectifier alone is sufficient against sign-flip: a gradient pointed against the server's reference scores negative and is gated to $w_{\mathrm{adv}} = 0.0000$, with training intact. Unweighted FedAvg under the same attack diverges on all ten seeds.

Second, under fairness poisoning the rectifier admits a fraction: $w_{\mathrm{adv}} = 0.0309$ against a uniform share of $0.20$. Removing the server holdout tier (M6) triples that to $0.0916$, and the corresponding disparity rises significantly ($\Delta\dpd_{\mathrm{hard}} = +0.1019$ against M1, paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.0020$, $n = 10$). This is the clearest evidence we have that the second tier earns its place, and we note that it is attack-specific: the same contrast under gradient scaling is null ($p = 0.6406$).

Third---and this is the break---gradient scaling defeats the rectifier, at $w_{\mathrm{adv}} = 0.2008$ with two seeds diverging. The reason follows from the scope remark after Theorem~\ref{thm:metadata_immunity}: a scaling adversary aligns \emph{positively} with the reference and merely inflates magnitude, so a sign-based gate has nothing to catch. The median screen closes it: \texttt{robust\_fu\_shapley} is the only arm in the table holding $w_{\mathrm{adv}} = 0.0000$ across all four scenarios with no diverged seed. Across the wider ratio sweep it holds at every $f/K \in \{0.1,0.2,0.3\}$ on $5/5$ seeds, while the non-robust arm holds at none.

Fourth, EMA is not cosmetic. Removing it (M7) raises $w_{\mathrm{adv}}$ to $0.3410$ under scaling and, more seriously, destabilises training outright: six of ten seeds produce an undefined AUC under sign-flip and four of ten under scaling. We state this as divergence rather than as a variance-reduction percentage, because divergence is what the seeds show.

Table~\ref{tab:adaptive_poisoner} sweeps ten aggregation rules against the projected stealth adversary of Eq.~\eqref{eq:stealth} on Bail. At the highest corruption ratio tested, coordinate median holds disparity at $0.038$ and the FU-Shapley arms at $0.016$, while the rules that consume a per-client reported score degrade further---$0.053$ for CGSV, $0.066$ for BFWA, $0.110$ for Krum, and $0.130$ for the weighted BFWA variant, the worst cell in the table. The ordering is the paper's thesis in miniature: what is damaged is precisely what reads self-reported scores. This sweep is a single-seed diagnostic across four ratios and we attach no significance claim to individual cells.

\subsection{How faithful is the contribution score to exact Shapley?}
\label{sec:res_shapley}

\input{tables/trust.tex}

Not faithful enough to be called an approximation, and we tested this rather than assuming it. Against exact Shapley values over $125$ probe points, the pooled Pearson correlation is $0.7436$ and the pooled Spearman $0.6897$, against a pre-registered threshold of $0.80$; sign agreement is $73.6\%$ against a $85\%$ threshold, and the mean simplex $L_1$ distance is $0.7554$ against a target of $0.15$. Hypothesis H3 is refuted.

\begin{quote}
We therefore do not claim that $\varphi_k$ approximates the exact Shapley value. It is a first-order ranking signal costing $O(KP)$ against $O(2^K P)$, and the contribution of Pillar C2 is the removal of the metadata dependence (Theorem~\ref{thm:metadata_immunity}), not fidelity to a cooperative-game solution concept. We report the refutation because a mechanism named after Shapley invites exactly this question.
\end{quote}

\subsection{What does it cost, and does it scale?}
\label{sec:res_cost}

\input{tables/cost.tex}

On Pokec-z, TrustFedGNN takes $73.2$\,s per run against FedAvg-GCN's $35.5$\,s, a $2.06\times$ overhead measured with every arm on the same T4 in a single campaign. The clean no-FSER arm sits at $72.0$\,s, which localises essentially all of the overhead in the server-side scoring pass and the deep-network scaffold rather than in FSER. \subsection{Trust governance}
\label{sec:governance}

\input{tables/revision/trust_score_sensitivity.tex}

The contribution decomposition of Proposition~\ref{prop:decomposition} gives the operator a per-client, per-round record of why each participant was weighted as it was, split into utility and fairness terms. Around it we report MC-dropout epistemic uncertainty \cite{gal2016dropout}, expected calibration error, and a composite trust score aggregating five axes.

Rank stability of that composite score was tested by Monte Carlo over $2{,}000$ weight configurations, in two regimes, both of which we report. Under $\pm25\%$ perturbation of the sub-score weights, Spearman $\rho_s = 0.965$ with $100\%$ rank-1 retention; under an unconstrained Dirichlet$(\bm{1})$ prior---which permits weight vectors no operator would choose---$\rho_s = 0.777$ with $98.9\%$ retention. Quoting only the first regime would be selective reporting.

The operator-facing controls are $\tau$, $\epsilon$ and $\alpha$, each a first-class reportable quantity, which is what makes the system auditable against EU AI Act Articles 10, 13, 14 and 15 and the NIST AI RMF. Attention weights are used here as behavioural diagnostics; we do not present them as sample-level causal explanations.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Seven hypotheses were pre-registered before the runs and refuted by them. Reporting them is not a concession; a fairness paper whose every hypothesis confirmed would warrant more suspicion, not less.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{FSER does not improve utility (P1).} $\Delta\mathrm{AUC} = -0.0017$ at $p = 0.2324$ on Pokec-z, winning $3/10$ seeds. Its measured value is as a safe inductive prior with a zero-distortion limit, not as a source of gain (Section~\ref{sec:res_attribution}).

\item \textbf{FSER does not improve benign fairness.} On German, $\Delta\dpd_{\mathrm{hard}} = +0.0090$ at $p = 0.7344$: null on both axes in benign operation.

\item \textbf{We are not fairest, and on one benchmark we are dominated (P3).} On Pokec-z our $\dpd_{\mathrm{hard}}$ is second-worst of ten rows and our EOD worst. On Credit Default, FedAvg-GCN is nominally ahead on both axes, though both margins lie inside noise. The paper's defensible claims are the three guarantees---statistic-level privacy, Byzantine resilience, and incentive compatibility---not superiority on group fairness.

\item \textbf{One component has no empirical support, and one is supported only against one attack (P5, P6).} Both the two-tier holdout (M6) and FairScore (M5) are null in benign operation. Under attack they separate. Removing the two-tier holdout significantly worsens disparity under fairness poisoning ($\Delta\dpd_{\mathrm{hard}} = +0.1019$, $p = 0.0020$) and triples the adversary's weight, so that tier is supported---but only against that attack; under gradient scaling the measured effect is null and runs in the wrong direction ($p = 0.6406$). FairScore is null under both attacks ($p = 0.7422$ scaling, $p = 0.7695$ fairness poisoning) and in the wrong direction. We retain FairScore for the formal decomposition it provides (Proposition~\ref{prop:decomposition}) and state plainly that we have no empirical evidence it improves any measured outcome. EMA, by contrast, has clear evidence (Section~\ref{sec:res_byzantine}).

\item \textbf{Fairness under attack is directionally right but inconclusive (P8$'$).} Disparity under fairness poisoning is $0.0665$ against FedAvg's $0.1605$, but at $n = 10$ this gives $p = 0.3750$ and does not meet the pre-registered threshold. We report it as an inconclusive observation, and decline to substitute the $2.4\times$ ratio for the $p$-value.

\item \textbf{The homophily hypothesis is not established (P10).} FSER benefit scaling with $h_s$ gives $p = 0.0583$ with two of four points null.

\item \textbf{The contribution score is not a Shapley approximation (H3).} Pooled $r = 0.7436 < 0.80$ (Section~\ref{sec:res_shapley}).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Four further boundaries are structural. The DP certificate covers the released statistic only, leaving a measured $0.645$ residual on the update channel. The robustness guarantee assumes $f < K/2$ and an adversary without full control of the transport. The attack sweeps use a single intensity ($10.0$), at which an inner-product attack becomes $-10\times$ the mean update and is arguably easy to detect; we did not sweep intensity, and a weaker, subtler adversary is untested. Finally, the two benchmarks held to the full ten-seed, seven-baseline protocol are mid-scale ($67.8$k and $30$k nodes); extending that protocol to the remaining benchmarks is future work.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Fairness-aware federated aggregation has been built, almost universally, on statistics that clients report about themselves. That design makes the fairness mechanism the softest target in the protocol: a participant that claims to be fair is rewarded for the claim. TrustFedGNN removes the dependence rather than hardening it. The server manufactures its own bi-objective reference gradient on a holdout split no client can reach, scores each participant by alignment with it, and gates those scores onto the simplex. Falsifying a report is then not merely detectable but inert---a client that lies leaves the weight vector bit-identical, while the same lie hands it $86\%$ of a Frank--Wolfe aggregate.

Around that core, statistic-level differential privacy exploits the fact that the sensitive attribute enters the objective through exactly two scalars, buying a certified $(\epsilon,\delta)$ release for $O(1)$ noise and $0.0017$ AUC where full-parameter DP-SGD costs $0.2016$. Four algebraic properties of the weight rule are machine-checked in Lean 4. On a real social graph the method attains the best utility among seven external baselines with every pairwise test surviving correction.

We are equally clear about what the evidence does not support. The component the architecture is named for contributes nothing measurable to utility or benign fairness; the utility margin comes from the aggregation rule and a standard scaffold. The contribution score is not a faithful Shapley approximation, and we refute rather than assert that. Our disparity is not best-in-class on either flagship benchmark. The privacy certificate stops at the statistic channel. What remains, and what we claim, is narrower and firmer than the pillar-level description might suggest: among the methods surveyed, this is the only one holding all four trust properties at an acceptable level simultaneously, and the only one whose fairness-aware aggregation cannot be steered by a client's account of itself.

\subsection*{Future work}
The most consequential gap is theoretical: FU-Shapley has no convergence analysis, and its stability rests on the empirical behaviour of the EMA recursion. Closing the residual update channel calls for composition with secure aggregation or a full-update DP variant redesigned to tolerate FSER's dependence on $\bm{s}$. On the empirical side, sweeping attack intensity downward toward the detectability boundary would test the defence where it is least characterised, and extending the ten-seed, seven-baseline protocol to the remaining benchmarks would broaden the utility claim. Finally, the fairness--homophily interaction suggests replacing the edge-level penalty with a mechanism that adapts to the measured $h_s$ of the deployment graph, rather than assuming topological signal exists.
