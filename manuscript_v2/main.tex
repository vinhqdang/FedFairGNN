\begin{abstract}
Federated learning on graph neural networks (Federated GNNs) across distributed, privacy-sensitive environments faces a critical structural vulnerability: existing fairness-aware and contribution-based aggregation protocols invariably rely on client-reported scalar telemetry or pooled validation graphs. In this work, we demonstrate empirically that across six representative fairness-aware federated rules, malicious clients submitting falsified disparity claims capture disproportionate aggregation weights (up to $97.4\%$ model control), severely corrupting downstream demographic parity and predictive utility. Furthermore, we establish via an information-theoretic Minimax lower bound (Le Cam's two-point method with Pinsker's inequality) that privatising self-reported statistics via Local Differential Privacy (LDP) inevitably collapses server observability ($\mathcal{R}^* \ge 43.5\%$ at $\epsilon = 8.0$, rising to $\ge 49.0\%$ at $\epsilon = 1.0$) due to perpetual folded-normal bias. To resolve this structural impasse, we propose \MethodName{}, a server-anchored federated graph learning framework operating under strict Kerckhoffs security assumptions. By deriving a dual-objective reference vector on an isolated server holdout graph ($D_{\mathrm{root}}$) and filtering client updates via non-linear coordinate gating (EMA smoothing, non-linear rectification, and dynamic norm rescaling), \MethodName{} establishes exact syntactic metadata immunity by construction ($\Delta w \equiv 0$). Across extensive evaluations on four real-world graph benchmarks (German Credit, Bail Recidivism, Credit Default, and Pokec-z) under three tiers of adversarial attacks, \MethodName{} restricts stealth adversaries below equal share ($0.68\times$--$0.73\times$) without imposing any robustness tax on group fairness. Finally, \MethodName{} incorporates an auditable, linear-complexity first-order contribution decomposition $O(KP)$ for per-round regulatory compliance logging, paired with a multi-criteria governance ranking whose stability is empirically preserved across 2,000 Monte Carlo weight configurations ($\rho_s = 0.965$), effectively bridging robust federated graph learning with enterprise risk governance.
\end{abstract}
