\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Fair graph neural networks}
Centralised fair GNNs remove sensitive information from representations or rebalance training: FairGNN~\citep{dai2021say} uses an adversary to estimate and remove the attribute, NIFTY~\citep{agarwal2021towards} enforces counterfactual stability, EDITS~\citep{dong2022edits} debiases attributes and structure, and FairSIN~\citep{yang2024fairsin} neutralises sensitive information through added features; re-balancing~\citep{li2024fairgb} and attribute-agnostic methods~\citep{zhu2024fairinv} follow the same goal. All of them assume one party holds the whole graph and the attribute. We use FairGNN and FairSIN as baselines, trained with federated averaging.

\paragraph{Fairness in federated learning}
FairFed~\citep{ezzeldin2023fairfed} reweights clients by the gap between their local fairness and the global fairness, q-FFL~\citep{li2020fair} reweights by local loss, and FedFACT~\citep{fedfact2025} calibrates a trained model to a group-fairness target with provable control. PUFFLE~\citep{corbucci2024puffle} and FedFDP~\citep{ling2024feddp} combine fairness with differential privacy. FedFDP targets \emph{performance} fairness across clients rather than group fairness, which we note when we compare against it. For graphs, F$^2$GNN~\citep{f2gnn2023} combines structure-aware local fairness with fairness-weighted aggregation, FairGFL~\citep{fairgfl2026} handles overlapping subgraphs under privacy, and FedGraph-Fair~\citep{fedgraphfair2026} uses personalisation and distributionally robust optimisation. These methods either read per-client fairness or loss values on the server or assume honest clients. \clamp{} uses only validated sums and is designed for a Byzantine minority.

\paragraph{Constrained learning}
Enforcing fairness as a constraint through a Lagrangian is standard in centralised learning~\citep{cotter2019optimization}. Our dual update is the simplest form of this approach, with one multiplier. What is new is where the information comes from: the multiplier is driven by statistics that are privatised, aggregated under secure aggregation and partly controlled by an adversary, and we bound the effect of each of these on the estimate.

\paragraph{Byzantine robustness and secure aggregation}
Robust aggregation rules (Krum~\citep{blanchard2017krum}, coordinate median and trimmed mean~\citep{yin2018byzantine}, Bulyan~\citep{mhamdi2018hidden}) and trust-scored rules such as FLTrust~\citep{cao2021fltrust} require access to individual updates, and they can be evaded by attacks that stay within the spread of honest updates~\citep{baruch2019little,xie2019fall}. Secure aggregation~\citep{bonawitz2017practical} hides individual updates, which rules these defences out. Verifiable aggregation systems such as Prio~\citep{corrigangibbs2017prio} and RoFL~\citep{lycklama2023rofl} restore a limited form of robustness by proving norm and range constraints on encrypted inputs. \clamp{} builds on exactly this capability: its only robustness mechanism is a norm and range check, and we show what that check guarantees for both the model and the fairness estimate. Distributed differential privacy with secure aggregation~\citep{kairouz2021distributed} provides the noise mechanism we assume in deployment.

\paragraph{Positioning}
Previous methods for federated graph learning are fair, private or robust, and the ones that combine two properties mostly do so with mechanisms that inspect individual clients. A closely related line combines fairness-aware federated aggregation with cryptographic protection of the reports: Bendoukha et al.~\citep{bendoukha2025popets} compute FairFed's weights under homomorphic encryption, which hides the reports but still uses per-client fairness values and assumes honest clients. Byzantine-resilient secure aggregation protocols such as BREA~\citep{so2021byzantine} and ELSA~\citep{rathee2023elsa} provide stronger update-level robustness under encryption than a norm check, at a higher cryptographic cost, but do not address fairness. \clamp{} is a simpler point in this space: it obtains fairness control, a privacy guarantee for the statistics and a bound on per-round update influence from validated sums alone, and our experiments show where these properties are weak. Our own earlier conference work~\citep{dang2026fedfairgnn} addressed privacy and fairness for fraud detection with client-side mechanisms and did not consider Byzantine clients or secure aggregation.
