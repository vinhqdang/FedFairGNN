\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked what a federation can achieve for group fairness, privacy and robustness if its server only ever sees validated sums. \clamp{} answers with three ingredients: clipped secure aggregation of updates, four privatised group statistics per release computed without reading the sensitive attribute, and a single broadcast Lagrange multiplier, completed by an output rule that returns a model whose released gap met the budget. Each ingredient comes with a guarantee whose scope is stated: attribute-level differential privacy of the statistics, a per-round bound on Byzantine influence on the model, a bound on Byzantine influence on the fairness estimate, a fairness guarantee for the returned model on honest validation data, and an a-posteriori certificate of average constraint violation.

On the medium and large graphs where the budget binds, \clamp{} holds the test gap at or near the budget at the utility of an unconstrained model, and the budget is a usable control knob. The experiments also show the limits of the sums-only design. Methods that read per-client fairness information in the clear reach lower gaps; on graphs with a few hundred nodes the privatised statistics are too noisy for effective control; demographic-parity control does not remove the sensitive attribute from the learned representation; and a single client that falsifies its statistics can switch the controller off, although in our runs it could not push the gap above the unconstrained level. Robust aggregation of the fairness statistics that works on sums alone is the most important direction this work leaves open.
