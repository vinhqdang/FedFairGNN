\begin{abstract}
Federated graph neural networks are trained by institutions that cannot pool their graphs, and the resulting models should be fair across demographic groups, should not expose sensitive attributes, and should withstand a minority of compromised clients. Existing methods provide these properties with mechanisms that inspect individual clients, which the server must be trusted with and an adversary can target. We present \clamp{}, a protocol in which the server only ever sees validated sums. Clients clip their model updates and report four group statistics computed without reading the sensitive attribute; secure aggregation with norm and range checks sums them, Gaussian noise privatises the statistics, and a single Lagrange multiplier broadcast to all clients enforces a global demographic-parity budget. The server returns the latest model, after a warm-up, whose released gap met the budget. We prove attribute-level differential privacy for validation nodes in the statistics channel, a per-round bound on the influence of Byzantine updates, a bound on the influence of falsified statistics on the fairness estimate, and a fairness guarantee for the returned model on honest validation data, and we state the scope of each; the two Byzantine bounds are loose, and in many settings vacuous. On eight benchmarks with ten seeds against eleven baselines, \clamp{} brings the demographic-parity gap from 0.071, 0.042 and 0.052 to 0.018, 0.026 and 0.028 on the three graphs where the budget of 0.03 binds, at an AUC within 0.01 of an unconstrained clipped model, and the budget acts as a control knob within a dataset-dependent range. Baselines that read per-client fairness information in the clear reach lower gaps. On small graphs the private statistics are too noisy for effective control, and a single client that falsifies its statistics can switch the controller off.
\end{abstract}
