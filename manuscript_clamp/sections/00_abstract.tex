\begin{abstract}
Federated graph neural networks are trained by institutions that cannot pool their graphs, and the resulting models must be fair across demographic groups, must not expose sensitive attributes, and must withstand a minority of compromised clients. Existing methods provide these properties with mechanisms that inspect individual clients, which the server must be trusted with and an adversary can target. We present \clamp{}, a protocol in which the server only ever sees validated sums. Clients clip their model updates and report four group statistics computed without reading the sensitive attribute; secure aggregation with norm and range checks sums them, Gaussian noise privatises the statistics, and a single Lagrange multiplier broadcast to all clients enforces a global demographic-parity budget. The server returns the latest model whose released gap met the budget. We prove attribute-level differential privacy of the statistics channel, a per-round bound on the influence of Byzantine updates, a bound on the influence of falsified statistics on the fairness estimate, a fairness guarantee for the returned model, and an a-posteriori certificate of constraint violation, and we state what each result does not cover. On eight benchmarks with ten seeds against eleven baselines, \clamp{} holds the demographic-parity gap at the chosen budget on the medium and large graphs where the budget binds, at the utility of an unconstrained model, and the budget acts as a control knob. The experiments also expose the limits of the design: on small graphs the private statistics are too noisy for effective control, and one client that falsifies its statistics can switch the controller off.
\end{abstract}
