\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Graph neural networks (GNNs)~\citep{kipf2016semi,velickovic2017graph} are used for credit scoring, bail decisions and social recommendation, all settings in which the data are split across institutions and the predictions affect people. Federated learning~\citep{mcmahan2017communication} lets such institutions train one model without pooling their graphs. Three requirements then arise together. The model should not discriminate between demographic groups~\citep{dai2021say,agarwal2021towards}. The federation should not learn more about any client, or about any person's sensitive attribute, than it needs to~\citep{abadi2016deep,mcmahan2018learning}. And a few compromised clients should not be able to steer the model~\citep{blanchard2017krum,yin2018byzantine,baruch2019little}.

These requirements are usually addressed one or two at a time, and the mechanisms do not compose. Fair federated methods reweight clients or calibrate the model using per-client fairness or loss reports~\citep{ezzeldin2023fairfed,li2020fair,fedfact2025}; the server must read those reports, and a Byzantine client can forge them to capture weight or to hide unfairness. Byzantine-robust aggregation rules such as the coordinate median, the trimmed mean and Krum~\citep{yin2018byzantine,blanchard2017krum} need to see individual updates, so they cannot run under secure aggregation~\citep{bonawitz2017practical}, the standard tool for hiding individual updates from the server. Private fair methods~\citep{corbucci2024puffle,ling2024feddp} add noise to gradients but assume every client is honest. In our earlier work on federated GNNs for fraud detection~\citep{dang2026fedfairgnn} we combined privacy and fairness through client-side mechanisms, and the same tension appears there: every component that inspects one client at a time is a component the server must be trusted with and an adversary can target.

This paper takes a different route. We ask what can be achieved if the server is only ever allowed to see \emph{sums over clients}, each of whose summands is \emph{range-checked}. We show that this restriction is enough to enforce a global group-fairness constraint, to privatise the information that the constraint needs, and to bound the influence of Byzantine clients on both the model and the fairness estimate. The resulting protocol, \clamp{}, has three components:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Clipped secure aggregation of updates.} Clients clip their updates to norm $C$, and secure aggregation with a validity check rejects any update above the bound. The server sees only the sum.
\item \textbf{Private group statistics.} With each release, clients report four scalars: per group, the sum of predictions and the count of validation nodes. These are computed with a forward pass that never reads the sensitive attribute, so their sensitivity is a constant, and they are summed under secure aggregation with Gaussian noise.
\item \textbf{One global Lagrange multiplier.} From the noisy sums, the server estimates the global demographic-parity gap and updates a single multiplier by projected dual ascent. It broadcasts the multiplier and the sign of the gap. Clients add a signed penalty to their local loss.
\end{itemize}
The design has no per-client weights, scores or reputations, so there is nothing for an adversary to capture over time, and every server-side computation is compatible with secure aggregation.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item We formulate fairness-constrained federated graph learning under a server that sees only validated sums and an adversary that controls up to $f$ clients' updates \emph{and} fairness statistics (Section~\ref{sec:problem}).
\item We propose \clamp{}, a protocol that enforces a global demographic-parity budget through a single broadcast multiplier computed from privatised aggregate statistics (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\item We prove four guarantees with explicit scope (Section~\ref{sec:guarantees}): differential privacy of the sensitive attribute in the statistics channel, a per-round bound $2fC/(K-f)$ on Byzantine influence on the model, a bound on Byzantine influence on the fairness estimate that depends only on public constants and the honest validation mass, and an a-posteriori certificate of average constraint violation. We state what each guarantee does not cover.
\item We evaluate \clamp{} on four fairness benchmarks against eleven baselines with ten seeds and paired significance tests, and under update attacks, statistics-falsification attacks, a range of privacy budgets, and ablations (Sections~\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:results}). The results show where the constraint is met, what it costs in utility, and where the approach is weak: on small graphs the noisy statistics limit control, and the clipping that bounds Byzantine influence also costs some accuracy.
\end{enumerate}
