\section{Experimental setup}\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Datasets}
We use eight public benchmarks for fair node classification (Table~\ref{tab:data}), all with a genuine binary sensitive attribute. \emph{German} (credit risk; gender) and \emph{Credit} (credit default; age) are similarity graphs over individuals, and \emph{Bail} (bail decisions; race) links defendants with similar records~\citep{agarwal2021towards}. \emph{Pokec-z} and \emph{Pokec-n} (region) are the two regional subsets of the Pokec social network used in~\citep{dai2021say}, and \emph{NBA} (nationality) links basketball players~\citep{dai2021say}. \emph{Facebook} (gender) is ego network 107 of the Facebook social circles data~\citep{mcauley2012learning}, and \emph{AMiner-S} is a co-authorship network whose sensitive attribute is the continent of the author's affiliation; we take both from the PyGDebias collection~\citep{dong2023fairness}. For AMiner-S the attribute has seven values, which we binarise as the most frequent continent against the rest, and we reduce the 5{,}693 bag-of-words features to 256 dimensions with truncated SVD (fixed random state) to keep full-batch training tractable; the label is the most frequent research field against the rest, as in PyGDebias. The sensitive attribute is removed from the node features in every dataset. Each run draws a stratified 50/25/25 train/validation/test split of the labelled nodes and partitions the nodes over $K=5$ clients with a Dirichlet($\alpha=0.3$) split over labels, which produces strongly non-IID clients. Each client keeps the subgraph induced by its nodes, so cross-client edges are lost (Table~\ref{tab:data}). The split, the partition and the model initialisation all depend on the seed.

\paragraph{Protocol constants}
All methods train for $T=100$ rounds with $E=3$ full-batch local epochs, AdamW with learning rate $0.01$ and weight decay $10^{-5}$, two-layer backbones with 64 hidden units (GAT: 4 heads) and dropout $0.3$. For \clamp{} we use $C=1$, $\eta=0.5$, fairness budget $\tau=0.03$, statistics privacy $\varepsilon=8$ at $\delta=10^{-5}$ for Theorem~\ref{thm:dp}, and release period $P=1$. On the three small graphs (German, NBA, Facebook) we use $P=10$; this choice was made before the experiments, on the expectation that per-round noise would dominate there, and the privacy sweep in the supplementary material shows that it is not better than $P=1$ on German for most budgets. These constants were fixed before the main experiments and were not tuned per dataset. The output rule itself was added after a first analysis showed that the last iterate oscillates around the budget (the ``last iterate'' rows of Table~\ref{tab:e1_main_compact} report the original method), and it uses a warm-up of $W=\lceil0.2T\rceil=20$ rounds. We added the warm-up after observing that without it the rule sometimes returned the barely trained round-1 model. Because the per-round test metrics are logged, the rule with warm-up was applied exactly to all runs; it changed 49 of the runs, which are listed in the released results. $\ncap$ must be public (Section~\ref{sec:guarantees}). The main experiments computed the cap per group, as the largest per-client validation group size (``group cap'' in Table~\ref{tab:data}), which depends on the attribute. This does not affect honest runs, because no report is truncated under either cap; the only place where the cap matters is the statistics-attack study, which we therefore run with the public cap $\ncap$. \clamp{} uses a GAT backbone, and \clamp{}-GCN swaps in GCN.

\paragraph{Baselines}
We compare with eleven baselines.
\begin{itemize}
\item FedAvg~\citep{mcmahan2017communication} with GCN and GAT backbones.
\item The fair GNNs FairGNN~\citep{dai2021say} and FairSIN~\citep{yang2024fairsin}, with their own backbones, trained with FedAvg.
\item The fair FL methods FairFed~\citep{ezzeldin2023fairfed} and FedFACT~\citep{fedfact2025} (its closed-form group mean-matching post-processing).
\item The federated graph methods F$^2$GNN~\citep{f2gnn2023} (GAT), FairGFL~\citep{fairgfl2026} (its aggregation rule) and FedGraph-Fair~\citep{fedgraphfair2026} (its distributionally robust reweighting, without personalisation).
\item The private fair methods PUFFLE~\citep{corbucci2024puffle} and FedFDP~\citep{ling2024feddp}.
\end{itemize}
FairFed, FairGFL, FedGraph-Fair, FedFACT, PUFFLE and FedFDP use a GCN backbone, as in their original federated settings. The appendix table lists the backbone of every method. FairFed, F$^2$GNN and FedFACT read per-client fairness quantities or group rates in the clear on the server; \clamp{} reads only noisy sums. PUFFLE and FedFDP use DP-SGD with privacy accounted per local step at $\varepsilon=8$ for the gradients, so their privacy unit (a client's training data) and ours (a validation node's attribute) differ, and the two values of $\varepsilon$ are not comparable. For FedFDP we clip at the batch level rather than per sample, because per-node gradients are not independent in a message-passing GNN. FedFDP optimises fairness of performance across clients, not group fairness, so it is not expected to reduce the demographic-parity gap. All baselines use the default hyperparameters of our implementations, which are documented in the released code; we did not tune them per dataset, just as we did not tune \clamp{}. Two ablations isolate the parts of \clamp{}. ClipMean is \clamp{} with $\eta=0$ and the last iterate, i.e.\ clipped secure aggregation without fairness control. \clamp{}-Select is \clamp{} with $\eta=0$ and the output rule, i.e.\ selection without a controller.

\paragraph{Metrics and statistics}
We report test AUC, the soft demographic-parity gap $\dpd$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpd} on test nodes, and, in the appendix, the hard demographic-parity gap and the equal-opportunity gap $\eod$ at threshold $0.5$, together with the certificate of Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} for every \clamp{} variant. The main comparison uses ten seeds per configuration. The sweeps use five seeds, and the attack studies and ablations use three. We test each baseline against \clamp{} with a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test paired by seed, with Holm correction across baselines within each dataset and metric. With ten seeds the smallest attainable Holm-adjusted $p$-value is about 0.03.

\paragraph{Attacks}
Update attacks (Section~\ref{sec:byz}) use $K=10$ clients of which $f\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ are Byzantine: sign flipping, scaling, ALIE~\citep{baruch2019little}, inner-product manipulation~\citep{xie2019fall} and a fairness-poisoning attack whose local loss rewards a larger demographic-parity gap while still fitting the labels. Under \clamp{} attacker updates are clipped to the norm bound $C$ like all others, so they always pass the validity check. We compare \clamp{} with FedAvg, the coordinate median, the trimmed mean and Krum, all with a GAT backbone; the last three need individual updates and cannot run under secure aggregation. Statistics attacks (Section~\ref{sec:statattack}) let the Byzantine clients train honestly but falsify their statistics with $N_a=\ncap$ and either swapped group rates (\emph{swap}) or both rates equal to the honest pooled rate (\emph{mid}), which drives the reported gap toward zero.

\paragraph{Compute}
German, Bail, NBA and Facebook, and the privacy studies, were run on a 4-core CPU. Credit, Pokec-z, Pokec-n and AMiner-S, and the sweep, attack and ablation studies on these graphs, were run on an NVIDIA T4 GPU. One run takes between 20 seconds (German, CPU) and about 14 minutes (Credit, CPU), or about 1--2 minutes on the GPU. The code, configurations and the raw results of every run, including per-round logs, will be made publicly available upon publication.
