\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked what a federation can achieve for group fairness, privacy and robustness if its server only ever sees validated sums. \clamp{} answers with three ingredients: clipped secure aggregation of updates, four privatised group statistics per release computed without reading the sensitive attribute, and a single broadcast Lagrange multiplier, completed by an output rule that returns a model whose released gap met the budget. Each ingredient comes with a result whose scope is stated: attribute-level differential privacy for validation nodes in the statistics channel, a per-round bound on Byzantine influence on the model, a bound on the influence of falsified statistics on the fairness estimate, a fairness guarantee for the returned model on honest validation data, and an a-posteriori record of the average estimated constraint violation. The two Byzantine bounds are loose, and the second is vacuous in most of the settings we evaluate.

On the three graphs where the budget binds (Credit, Pokec-z and Pokec-n), \clamp{} brings the test gap to or near the budget at an AUC within 0.01 of an unconstrained clipped model, and the budget is a usable control knob within a dataset-dependent range; on Bail it has no effect, and on the small graphs only a modest one. Under update attacks, clipping on sums is comparable to the coordinate-wise robust rules that need individual updates, and the controller counters a fairness-poisoning attack that neither those rules nor clipping alone stop. The experiments also show the limits of the sums-only design. Methods that read per-client fairness information in the clear reach lower gaps; on graphs with a few hundred nodes the validation statistics are too poor an estimate of the test gap for effective control, with or without privacy noise; demographic-parity control does not remove the sensitive attribute from the learned representation; and a single client that falsifies its statistics can switch the controller off, and with four liars out of ten it can, in some runs, drive the multiplier to a level that damages the model. Robust aggregation of the fairness statistics that works on sums alone is the most important direction this work leaves open.
