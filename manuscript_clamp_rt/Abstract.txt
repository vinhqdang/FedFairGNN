Fairness control in federated graph learning needs group statistics from the clients, and a client can falsify them. In an earlier protocol, CLAMP, the server pooled four range-checked group sums under secure aggregation; a single lying client out of ten raised the demographic-parity gap on Credit from 0.011 to 0.065, and the bound on that effect was vacuous. We present CLAMP-RT, which replaces the sums by a robust one-dimensional statistic. Each client secret-shares one scalar, its soft demographic-parity gap on validation nodes with a public group-size floor, between two non-colluding servers. The servers range-check, sort and trim the gaps in two-party computation, add Gaussian noise and release only the noisy trimmed mean, which drives a single Lagrange multiplier. We prove that with at most b liars the trimmed mean stays between two order statistics of the honest gaps, that the release is attribute-level differentially private with a sensitivity fixed by public validation-set sizes, and a high-probability bound on the released gap of the returned model. On four graphs with K in {5, 10} clients, four falsification attacks with f <= b liars changed the mean test gap by at most 0.009, while one liar beyond the breakdown point (f = b+1) let the hiding attack raise it to 0.11-0.30 on Credit and the Pokec graphs. A CrypTen two-party run of the gap channel took 0.3-1.1 s and at most 107 KB per release for K <= 20. The robustness has a price: with privacy noise on one trimmed scalar and ten releases, the honest gap is 0.037-0.061 on Credit and Pokec against 0.018-0.028 for CLAMP; on small graphs the noise exceeds the signal; and CLAMP-RT does not counter fairness poisoning through model updates.
