\begin{abstract}
Fairness control in federated graph learning needs group statistics from the clients, and a client can falsify them. In an earlier protocol, CLAMP, the server pooled four range-checked group sums under secure aggregation; a single lying client out of ten raised the demographic-parity gap on Credit from 0.011 to 0.065, and the bound on that effect was vacuous. We present \rt{}, which replaces the sums by a robust one-dimensional statistic. Each client secret-shares one scalar, its soft demographic-parity gap on validation nodes with a public group-size floor, between two non-colluding servers. The servers range-check, sort and trim the gaps in two-party computation, add Gaussian noise and release only the noisy trimmed mean, which drives a single Lagrange multiplier. We prove that with at most $b$ liars the trimmed mean stays between two order statistics of the honest gaps, that the release is attribute-level differentially private with a sensitivity fixed by public validation-set sizes, and a high-probability bound on the released gap of the returned model, whose radius is 0.19--0.59 on five graphs and vacuous on the three small ones. On four graphs with $K\in\{5,10\}$ clients, five falsification attacks, including an adaptive one that chooses the claim minimising the trimmed mean, left the mean test gap within 0.009 of the honest run when $f\le b$. With ten releases the honest run was itself close to training without control on the Pokec graphs; with per-round releases the multiplier was active and attacks with $f\le b$ again stayed within 0.006 of the honest level. One liar beyond the breakdown point let the hiding and adaptive attacks raise the gap to 0.10--0.30. A full release round in semi-honest CrypTen took 1.1--10.5\,s, dominated by the norm checks on the updates; the trimmed-mean part took at most 1.1\,s and 107\,KB. With privacy noise and ten releases, the honest gap is 0.037--0.061 on Credit and Pokec against 0.018--0.028 for CLAMP; on small graphs the noise exceeds the signal; and \rt{} does not counter fairness poisoning through model updates.
\end{abstract}
