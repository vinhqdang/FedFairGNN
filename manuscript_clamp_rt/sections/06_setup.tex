\section{Experimental setup}\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Datasets}
We use the eight public benchmarks of the earlier study (Table~\ref{tab:data}), all with a genuine binary sensitive attribute. \emph{German} (credit risk; gender) and \emph{Credit} (credit default; age) are similarity graphs over individuals, and \emph{Bail} (bail decisions; race) links defendants with similar records~\citep{agarwal2021towards}. \emph{Pokec-z} and \emph{Pokec-n} (region) are the regional subsets of the Pokec social network used in~\citep{dai2021say}, and \emph{NBA} (nationality) links basketball players~\citep{dai2021say}. \emph{Facebook} (gender) is ego network 107 of the Facebook social circles data~\citep{mcauley2012learning}, and \emph{AMiner-S} is a co-authorship network whose attribute is the continent of the author's affiliation (most frequent continent against the rest), taken from PyGDebias~\citep{dong2023fairness}, with features reduced to 256 dimensions by truncated SVD. The sensitive attribute is removed from the features. Each run draws a stratified 50/25/25 train/validation/test split and partitions the nodes over $K$ clients with a Dirichlet($\alpha=0.3$) split over labels; each client keeps the subgraph induced by its nodes. The split, the partition and the initialisation depend on the seed.

\paragraph{Protocol constants}
All methods train for $T=100$ rounds with $E=3$ full-batch local epochs, AdamW (learning rate $0.01$, weight decay $10^{-5}$), two-layer backbones with 64 hidden units (GAT with 4 heads) and dropout $0.3$. \rt{} uses $C=1$, $\eta=0.5$, $\tau=0.03$, $W=20$, $b=\max(1,\lfloor0.2K\rfloor)$, $c=0.05$, $P=10$ and $\varepsilon=8$, $\delta=10^{-5}$ for Theorem~\ref{thm:dp}, on every dataset. The resulting $n_{0,\min}$ and $\sigma_d$ are listed in Table~\ref{tab:channel}. The constants $C,\eta,\tau,W$ are those of \clamp{}. The values of $c$ and $P$ were chosen in pilot runs on Credit, Pokec-z and Bail (the design started from $c=0.1$; we also tried and discarded exponential smoothing of the released gap). The bias correction was added after the first full honest runs, which are reported as the row ``no bias correction'' in Table~\ref{tab:e1}, and was piloted on six datasets including the three small graphs. The pilots used seeds 0--2, which are among the reported seeds, so the reported results are not fully independent of these choices. All pilots, including the discarded variants (smoothing, a step scaled by the release period, a tighter controller target), are listed in Table~S5 of the supplementary material. Tables~\ref{tab:e5} and~\ref{tab:e6} report the other values of $P$, $b$ and $c$. No constant was tuned per dataset. \rt{} uses a GAT backbone and \rt{}-GCN a GCN backbone.

\paragraph{Simulation of the secure computation}
The training experiments execute the server-side arithmetic of Section~\ref{sec:method} in plaintext, in 64-bit floating point, with continuous Gaussian noise: validity checks, sort, trimming, noise and the masked update sum are computed exactly as specified, and the server code reads only the opened values. The cost of running the same steps in two-party computation is measured separately (Section~\ref{sec:mpc}). The fixed-point error of that implementation is below $\mpcErrMax\times10^{-5}$ on the trimmed mean, far below the noise, but the discrete noise a ring deployment would need is not part of either experiment.

\paragraph{Baselines}
The baseline runs are those of the earlier study, with the same splits, partitions and seeds: FedAvg~\citep{mcmahan2017communication} with GAT; the fair GNNs FairGNN~\citep{dai2021say} and FairSIN~\citep{yang2024fairsin} trained with FedAvg; FairFed~\citep{ezzeldin2023fairfed} and FedFACT~\citep{fedfact2025}; the federated graph methods F$^2$GNN~\citep{f2gnn2023}, FairGFL~\citep{fairgfl2026} and FedGraph-Fair~\citep{fedgraphfair2026}; and the private methods PUFFLE~\citep{corbucci2024puffle} and FedFDP~\citep{ling2024feddp}. FairFed, F$^2$GNN and FedFACT read per-client fairness quantities or group rates in the clear. PUFFLE and FedFDP account privacy per local step for gradients at $\varepsilon=8$; their privacy unit differs from ours and the two values of $\varepsilon$ are not comparable. ClipMean is clipped aggregation without a controller. The most important comparison is with \clamp{}, the sums-channel protocol: it differs from \rt{} only in the statistics channel, except that it released statistics every round ($P=1$) on Bail, Credit, Pokec-z, Pokec-n and AMiner-S and every ten rounds on the three small graphs. For \clamp{} we apply the same output-rule warm-up as for \rt{}, as in the earlier study.

\paragraph{Metrics and statistics}
We report test AUC and the soft demographic-parity gap $\dpd$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpd} on pooled test nodes, as mean $\pm$ standard deviation over seeds. The honest comparison (E1) uses ten seeds; attack studies, the privacy sweep and the ablations use three, so differences below about 0.01 in $\dpd$ are within seed noise there. For \rt{} against \clamp{} we report two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests paired by seed.

\paragraph{Experiments}
\emph{E1} (honest): eight datasets, $K=5$. \emph{E3} (update attacks): $K=10$, $f\in\{1,\dots,4\}$ Byzantine clients performing sign flipping, scaling, ALIE~\citep{baruch2019little}, inner-product manipulation~\citep{xie2019fall} or fairness poisoning, on Bail and Pokec-z; Byzantine clients report their true gaps. \emph{E4} (falsified gaps): Bail, Credit, Pokec-z and Pokec-n with $K=5$ ($f\in\{1,2\}$, $b=1$) and $K=10$ ($f\in\{1,2,3\}$, $b=2$), so that each setting includes one value of $f$ beyond the breakdown point. Liars train honestly and replace their gap by one of four omniscient claims within $[-1,1]$, computed from the honest gaps of the round with mean $g^H$: \emph{hide} reports $-\mathrm{sign}(g^H)$, \emph{inflate} reports $+\mathrm{sign}(g^H)$, \emph{inlier} reports the honest value closest to zero on the hiding side (the strongest claim that stays inside the honest range), and \emph{zero} reports $0$. For \clamp{} we reuse the earlier \emph{swap} attack on the four sums with the public cap. \emph{E5}: $\varepsilon\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ and $P\in\{1,5,10\}$ on Credit, Pokec-z and Pokec-n. \emph{E6}: ablations of $b$, $c$ and the output rule on Credit and Pokec-z.
