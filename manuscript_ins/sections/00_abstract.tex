\begin{abstract}
Federated graph neural networks let institutions train a shared model without pooling their graphs, but the model must also be fair across demographic groups, must not expose sensitive attributes, and must withstand compromised participants. Many fair federated methods read per-client fairness reports in the clear, which conflicts with secure aggregation and gives malicious clients an easy target. We present \clamp{}, a protocol in which the server sees only norm- and range-checked sums. Clients clip their updates and report four group statistics computed without reading the sensitive attribute; the server privatises these statistics with Gaussian noise, drives a single broadcast Lagrange multiplier toward a demographic-parity budget, and returns the latest model that met the budget. We prove differential privacy of the statistics channel, a per-round bound on Byzantine influence, and a fairness guarantee for the returned model. On eight benchmarks against eleven baselines, on the three larger graphs whose unconstrained gap exceeds the budget, \clamp{} lowers the soft demographic-parity gap of unconstrained clipped training by 38--75\%, to a mean at or below the budget, at an AUC within 0.01. When two to four of ten clients poison fairness through their updates on Pokec-z, \clamp{} keeps the gap at 0.037--0.049, whereas FedAvg, the coordinate median, the trimmed mean and clipping alone let it rise to 0.11--0.20. Baselines that read client reports in the clear reach lower gaps, control is weak on three small graphs, and a client that falsifies its statistics can disable it.
\end{abstract}
