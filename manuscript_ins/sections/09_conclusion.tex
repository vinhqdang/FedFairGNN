\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied group-fairness control in federated graph learning when the server may only see validated sums. \clamp{} combines clipped secure aggregation of updates, four privatised group statistics computed without reading the sensitive attribute, and a single broadcast Lagrange multiplier, completed by an output rule that returns a model whose released gap met the budget. We proved differential privacy of the statistics channel for validation-node attributes, a per-round bound on Byzantine influence on the model, a bound on the influence of falsified statistics, and a fairness guarantee for the returned model on honest validation data.

On the three larger graphs whose unconstrained gap exceeds the budget, \clamp{} brought the mean soft demographic-parity gap to or below the budget at an AUC within 0.01 of unconstrained clipped training, and the budget set the operating point on two of them. In a three-seed attack study, clipping on sums was comparable to coordinate-wise rules that need individual updates on most update attacks, and on Pokec-z the controller limited a fairness-poisoning attack that those rules and clipping alone did not stop, although the gap stayed above the budget. Baselines that read client reports in the clear reach lower gaps. The main limitations are on small graphs, where validation statistics are too poor an estimate of the test gap, and against clients that falsify their statistics, which can switch the controller off; the cryptographic layer was simulated, not implemented. Robust aggregation of the fairness statistics on sums alone is the most important direction this work leaves open.
