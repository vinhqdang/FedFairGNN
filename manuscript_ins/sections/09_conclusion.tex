\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied group-fairness control in federated graph learning when the server may only see validated sums. \clamp{} combines clipped secure aggregation of updates, four privatised group statistics computed without reading the sensitive attribute, and a single broadcast Lagrange multiplier, completed by an output rule that returns a model whose released gap met the budget. We proved differential privacy of the statistics channel for validation-node attributes, a per-round bound on Byzantine influence on the model, a bound on the influence of falsified statistics, and a fairness guarantee for the returned model on honest validation data.

On the graphs where the budget binds, \clamp{} brought the demographic-parity gap to within the budget at an AUC within 0.01 of unconstrained clipped training, and the budget acted as a control knob. Clipping on sums was comparably robust to update attacks as coordinate-wise rules that need individual updates, and the controller countered a fairness-poisoning attack that those rules and clipping alone did not stop. The main limitations are on small graphs, where validation statistics are too poor an estimate of the test gap, and against clients that falsify their statistics, which can switch the controller off. Robust aggregation of the fairness statistics on sums alone is the most important direction this work leaves open.
