\section{Trustworthiness Analysis and Regulatory Compliance}
\label{sec:trust}

\paragraph{Composite trust score.}
Trustworthiness is multi-dimensional: a model that is accurate but unfair, or
fair but privacy-leaking or attack-fragile, is not trustworthy. We summarise
each method by a single interpretable score in $[0,1]$ that aggregates five
normalised sub-scores---utility (AUC), fairness ($1-\tfrac{\dpd+\eod}{2}/F_{\text{ref}}$),
privacy ($1-\epsilon/\epsilon_{\max}$), robustness (attack-retained AUC), and
calibration ($1-\mathrm{ECE}/\mathrm{ECE}_{\text{ref}}$)---via a weighted power
mean. Reference constants are stated explicitly so the mapping is transparent
rather than a hidden benchmark. Under the geometric-mean setting ($p{=}0$),
which penalises \emph{any} weak axis, FedFairGNN achieves the highest trust
score among all methods (Table~\ref{tab:trust}): baselines that excel on one
axis are penalised for ignoring the others, whereas FedFairGNN is the only
method competitive on all of utility, fairness, privacy, and robustness
simultaneously.

\paragraph{Explainability audit.}
Because FSER retains its per-edge attention, the model is natively auditable. We
measure the \emph{attention bias ratio}: the share of attention mass on
cross-group edges divided by their prevalence. A ratio below one---observed for
FedFairGNN---means the model deliberately attends to cross-group edges
\emph{less} than chance, directly evidencing that FSER suppresses the biased
message passing it targets. Gradient attribution of the demographic-parity gap
further localises which input features drive residual disparity, giving auditors
an actionable, decision-level explanation.

\paragraph{Human-in-the-loop control.}
Two budgets are exposed to a human operator: the fairness budget $\tau$ (the
maximum tolerated global disparity, enforced by BFWA) and the privacy budget
$\epsilon$ (enforced by FTGD's accountant). The Pareto frontier
(Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}) turns budget selection into an informed choice rather
than a hidden default---an operationalisation of meaningful human oversight.

\paragraph{Regulatory alignment.}
Table~\ref{tab:compliance} maps FedFairGNN's components to the EU AI Act
(Articles 10, 13, 14, 15) and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework
(Govern/Map/Measure/Manage). Data governance is met by keeping raw graphs local
and excluding $s$ from features; transparency by the FSER explanation and model
card; human oversight by the $\tau,\epsilon$ budgets; and accuracy/robustness by
robust aggregation and the DP guarantee. The framework auto-generates a
Mitchell-style model card, turning accountability into a reproducible artifact.
