\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

High-stakes decisions in finance and public administration---credit approval,
fraud and anti-money-laundering (AML) screening, pretrial risk---are
increasingly mediated by machine-learning models operating over
\emph{relational} data. Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) are the de facto tool for
these tasks because fraud, default, and recidivism propagate along
relationships between users, accounts, and transactions
\citep{kipf2016semi,velickovic2017graph,dou2020enhancing}. In practice the
relevant graph is fragmented across institutions---different banks, exchanges,
or agencies---each legally barred from pooling raw records under regimes such as
the GDPR. Federated Learning (FL) \citep{mcmahan2017communication} resolves this
by training a shared model through the exchange of model updates rather than
data.

However, deployability is no longer defined by accuracy alone. The European
Commission's Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI, the EU AI Act, and the U.S.
NIST AI Risk Management Framework all require that automated decision systems be
\emph{fair}, \emph{privacy-preserving}, \emph{robust}, \emph{transparent}, and
\emph{accountable}. Federated graph learning strains every one of these axes
simultaneously. \textbf{Fairness}: GNN message passing amplifies structural
bias, so that members of a demographic group connected to risky neighbours are
flagged disproportionately, and the server cannot measure this directly because
it never sees the data. \textbf{Privacy}: model updates leak information about
local records, yet the standard remedy---differentially private
SGD---injects isotropic noise across every parameter and, on the compact GNNs
used here, destroys utility (Section~\ref{sec:exp-privacy}). \textbf{Robustness}:
a single malicious or malfunctioning client can corrupt the global model, and
we show that fairness mechanisms open a \emph{new} attack surface---a client can
report fabricated fairness statistics to capture a fairness-aware aggregator.
\textbf{Transparency}: relational models are opaque, complicating the audits
that regulation demands.

Prior work treats these properties in isolation: fair GNNs assume centralised
data \citep{dai2021say,yang2024fairsin,lee2025dabgnn}; fair FL largely targets
tabular or image data \citep{ezzeldin2023fairfed,li2020fair}; robust aggregation
ignores fairness \citep{blanchard2017krum,yin2018byzantine}. To our knowledge no
method jointly delivers fairness, differential privacy, and Byzantine robustness
for federated GNNs, and none has been evaluated on large-scale crypto graphs.

We close this gap with \textbf{FedFairGNN}, a unified framework whose
contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Fairness-Sensitive Edge Reweighting (FSER)}: a learnable
attention correction that identifies and suppresses biased cross-group edges
during message passing, mitigating structural discrimination at its source
(Section~\ref{sec:fser}).

\item \textbf{Fairness-Targeted Gradient Decomposition (FTGD)}: the observation
that the sensitive attribute enters the model \emph{only} through the
demographic-parity statistics lets us obtain $(\epsilon,\delta)$-differential
privacy by privatising two bounded-sensitivity scalars rather than the full
gradient. This makes privacy essentially free---where DP-FedAvg collapses
utility---and we prove the guarantee via a self-contained R\'enyi-DP accountant
(Section~\ref{sec:ftgd}).

\item \textbf{Bi-objective Frank--Wolfe Aggregation (BFWA)} and a
\textbf{Byzantine-robust variant}: server-side constrained optimisation enforces
a hard fairness budget during aggregation, while distance-based screening
defends against poisoning---including a novel fairness-poisoning attack we
introduce (Sections~\ref{sec:bfwa},~\ref{sec:exp-robust}).

\item \textbf{A trustworthiness instrumentation layer}: predictive uncertainty
and calibration, GNN attention explanations with fairness attribution, a
transparent composite \emph{trust score}, sustainability accounting, and an
explicit EU~AI~Act / NIST~RMF compliance mapping with an auto-generated model
card (Sections~\ref{sec:method-trust},~\ref{sec:trust}).

\item \textbf{A comprehensive, reproducible evaluation} on five real datasets
spanning credit, recidivism, social, and large-scale crypto (Elliptic Bitcoin,
$203{,}769$ nodes) domains, against ten baselines including recent
fairness-aware federated and graph methods. FedFairGNN attains the best overall
trust trade-off; every reported number is regenerated from logged runs.
\end{enumerate}

By advancing trustworthy, infrastructure-grade collaborative AI, this work
supports UN Sustainable Development Goal~9. The remainder of the paper reviews
related work (Section~\ref{sec:related}), formalises the setting
(Section~\ref{sec:prelim}), details the method (Section~\ref{sec:method}),
reports experiments (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}), analyses trustworthiness
and compliance (Section~\ref{sec:trust}), and discusses limitations
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
