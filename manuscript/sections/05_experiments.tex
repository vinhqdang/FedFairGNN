\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\paragraph{Datasets.}
We use five real datasets with \emph{genuine} sensitive attributes
(Table~\ref{tab:datasets}): \textbf{German} (credit; gender), \textbf{Credit}
(default; age), and \textbf{Bail} (recidivism; race)---the standard fair-GNN
benchmarks used by our fairness baselines; \textbf{Pokec} (social; region); and
\textbf{Elliptic} (Bitcoin AML; $203{,}769$ transactions,
$234{,}355$ edges), a large-scale \emph{crypto} graph on which we are, to our
knowledge, the first to study group fairness, using an operational early/late
temporal subgroup as a documented proxy. Data are partitioned across clients by
a Dirichlet($\alpha{=}0.5$) label split with a small floor to avoid degenerate
silos.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare against ten methods spanning the axes reviewers expect: non-fair
backbones FedAvg-GCN/GAT; fair-GNN methods FairGNN \citep{dai2021say} and FairSIN
\citep{yang2024fairsin} (run federated); fairness-aware FL aggregation FairFed
\citep{ezzeldin2023fairfed}, q-FedAvg \citep{li2020fair}, FedFB, and the
federated fair-GNN F$^2$GNN \citep{f2gnn2023}; and the DP baseline DP-FedAvg
\citep{mcmahan2018learning}. Ours appears as FedFairGNN (with FTGD-DP),
FedFairGNN~(no~DP), and FedFairGNN-Robust.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
All methods share a 2-layer GNN ($d_h{=}64$), AdamW ($\eta{=}0.01$), $E{=}2$
local epochs, and dataset-specific rounds. We report mean\,$\pm$\,std over three
seeds on the pooled global test set. Unless stated, DP targets $\epsilon{=}8$,
$\delta{=}10^{-5}$; the fairness budget is $\tau{=}0.05$. Every table and figure
is regenerated from logged runs by \texttt{experiments/report.py}.

\subsection{Main comparison: fairness--utility}
\label{sec:exp-main}
Tables~\ref{tab:main-auc}--\ref{tab:main-eod} report utility (AUC), demographic
parity, and equal-opportunity gaps. FedFairGNN attains the strongest
\emph{fairness}--utility trade-off: it substantially reduces $\dpd$ and $\eod$
relative to the non-fair backbones while keeping AUC competitive with the best
baseline, and it does so \emph{under} an $(\epsilon{=}8)$-DP guarantee that the
other fairness methods do not provide. On Elliptic---beyond the reach of the
centralised fairness baselines---FedFairGNN delivers the best fairness at
comparable detection accuracy, demonstrating that trustworthy federated fraud
detection scales to hundreds of thousands of nodes.

\subsection{Privacy--utility: FTGD vs.\ full-gradient DP}
\label{sec:exp-privacy}
Figure~\ref{fig:privacy} sweeps the privacy budget $\epsilon$. FTGD retains
essentially full utility and fairness across $\epsilon\in[0.5,16]$, because it
privatises only the two-dimensional fairness statistic. In contrast, DP-FedAvg's
isotropic gradient noise collapses AUC toward chance at small $\epsilon$. This is
the central privacy result: \emph{FedFairGNN provides sensitive-attribute
differential privacy at negligible cost}, where the standard mechanism is
unusable on compact GNNs.

\subsection{Robustness to Byzantine and fairness-poisoning attacks}
\label{sec:exp-robust}
Table~\ref{tab:robustness} reports utility retained under Gaussian, ALIE, and
fairness-poisoning attacks with $2/10$ malicious clients, across aggregators;
Figure~\ref{fig:robust} sweeps the number of Byzantine clients. FedAvg and plain
BFWA collapse under attack, and---critically---plain BFWA is \emph{captured} by
the fairness-poisoning attack that fabricates fairness statistics.
FedFairGNN-Robust (robust\_bfwa) preserves utility and fairness by screening
malicious updates before applying the fairness constraint, matching or exceeding
dedicated robust aggregators while remaining fair.

\subsection{Uncertainty, calibration, and convergence}
Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} reports ECE, Brier score, and the group-conditional

uncertainty on one group. Figure~\ref{fig:convergence} shows stable joint
convergence of utility and both fairness metrics---FTGD's clean task pathway

\subsection{Scalability and sustainability}
Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} also reports communication and efficiency. FedFairGNN
transmits only model weights, so its per-round communication equals FedAvg's;
fairness and privacy therefore incur \emph{no} communication overhead. The
method scales to $K{=}20$ clients and to the $200$k-node Elliptic graph on
commodity CPU hardware.
